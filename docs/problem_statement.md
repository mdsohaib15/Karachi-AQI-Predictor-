
## Air Quality Index (AQI) 

### 1. What is AQI?

**Air Quality Index (AQI)** is a numerical scale used to indicate how clean or polluted the air is and what health effects may occur. It converts the concentration of different air pollutants into a single value that is easy to understand.

A typical AQI scale is:

* **0–50:** Good
* **51–100:** Moderate
* **101–150:** Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups
* **151–200:** Unhealthy
* **201–300:** Very Unhealthy
* **301–500:** Hazardous

---

### 2. Why is AQI Important?

AQI is important because it:

* Helps people understand current air quality.
* Warns the public about harmful pollution levels.
* Protects vulnerable groups such as children, elderly people, and asthma patients.
* Supports government and environmental agencies in pollution control.
* Assists in planning outdoor activities safely.

---

### 3. Which Pollutants Affect AQI?

The main pollutants used to calculate AQI are:

* **PM2.5** (Fine particulate matter)
* **PM10** (Coarse particulate matter)
* **Carbon Monoxide (CO)**
* **Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)**
* **Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂)**
* **Ozone (O₃)**

---

### 4. Who Will Use This System?

The AQI prediction system can be used by:

* General public
* Environmental protection agencies
* Government authorities
* Researchers and students
* Hospitals and healthcare organizations
* Weather forecasting services

---

### 5. What Should Your System Predict?

The system should predict:

* The **Air Quality Index (AQI)** value.
* The **AQI category** (Good, Moderate, Unhealthy, etc.).
* Optionally, the dominant pollutant responsible for poor air quality.

---
